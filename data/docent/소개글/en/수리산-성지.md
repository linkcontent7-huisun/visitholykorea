"Francis Choe Gyeong-hwan, father of Father Choe Yang-eop, came to Surisan with his family in 1838 and led the village of believers. Arrested with some forty villagers in the Gihae Persecution of 1839, he died in prison after torture by the police bureau, and his wife Yi Seong-rye was martyred the following year."

"The bottleneck-narrow valley of Surisan in Anyang was called Dambaegol or Byeongmokgol — tobacco valley, or bottleneck valley. The believers grew tobacco on the hillsides to escape the authorities' notice, and when persecution struck, the catechist Choe Gyeong-hwan recovered the martyrs' remains and cared for the scattered faithful."

"The shrine holds a small church restoring St. Choe Gyeong-hwan's old house, statues of the couple, and the graves on the mountain path. Climbing from the pilgrims' church along the rosary path and the Way of the Cross, the grave of Choe Gyeong-hwan and the Marian grotto show the terrain of the old village of believers."
