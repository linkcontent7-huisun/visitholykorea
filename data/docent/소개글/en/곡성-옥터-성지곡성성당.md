"Fleeing the Eulhae Persecution of 1815, believers from Gyeongsang and Gangwon hid in the mountains of Gokseong, firing earthenware to conceal their identity and keep the faith. When the Jeonghae Persecution broke out in 1827, they were arrested again and tortured in a makeshift prison made from the Gokseong county guesthouse. Many renounced the faith, but Magdalene Yi, John Yi Seong-ji and others held on to the end and were martyred."

"The shrine is in the town of Gokseong, Jeollanam-do, on the site of the Joseon-era county office and guesthouse where believers dragged down from the mountain villages were held. Today a quiet prison-site exhibition room behind the church bears witness to what happened here two centuries ago."

"In 1957, a hundred and thirty years after the martyrdom, the Archdiocese of Gwangju founded Gokseong parish on this very site to honor the faith of its forebears. Beside the church, a reconstructed prison cell, a Way of the Cross and a Marian garden show how ground that was once a jail became a yard for prayer."
