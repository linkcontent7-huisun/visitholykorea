"After the mission stations at Ganwol and Daejae were destroyed in the Byeongin Persecution, believers who fled founded the Salti mission station in 1868. The community deep in the mountains became the longest-surviving mission station in the Diocese of Busan and raised many clergy, including Bishop Choe Jae-seon."

"The Salti village of believers in Deokhyeon-ri, Sangbuk-myeon, Ulju, lay in deep forest beyond Seongnamsa temple, where tigers appeared. Because the mountain path made the authorities turn back toward Gyeongju, the believers survived — and so, it is said, the place came to be called the pass where one can live."

"The small station building and the mountain paths show the scale of a refugee community that has continued since 1868. Salti mission station carries on the memory of Daejae station and the Ganwol village, and remains a seedbed of vocations that produced a bishop, priests and religious for the Diocese of Busan."
