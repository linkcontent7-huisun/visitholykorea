"In 1784 the interpreter Thomas Kim Beom-u offered his house at Myeongnyebang so that Yi Seung-hun, Yi Byeok, the brothers Jeong Yak-jeon and Jeong Yak-yong, and Kwon Il-sin could meet. After the gathering was discovered by officials of the Hyeongjo in the Eulsa incident of 1785, Kim Beom-u was exiled and died the following year."

"The house site is in the Myeong-dong area of Jung-gu, Seoul, then the busy district called Myeongnyebang. Though now city center near Myeongdong Cathedral, it was the living space of Korea's first Catholic community, where laypeople gathered without a priest to learn doctrine and hold rites."

"Kim Beom-u's house no longer survives, so pilgrims trace it through a marker, also known as the site of the Jangagwon music office, and the streets of Myeong-dong. The Seoul pilgrimage route distinguishes this house site from the founding site of the Korean Catholic Church, remembering the meeting place and the founding event separately."
