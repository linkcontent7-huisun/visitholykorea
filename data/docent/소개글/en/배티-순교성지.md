"Baeti is a village of believers who, from around 1830, hid in valley after valley to escape persecution. Before the Byeongin Persecution there were more than ten hamlets — Sambakgol, Jeolgol, Yongjingol, Ballaegi and others — and people call the area the catacombs of Korea. The Byeongin Persecution of 1866 and the Mujin Persecution of 1868 produced fifty-five martyrs here, twenty-six of whom remain in nameless graves."

"The shrine lies in a deep valley beneath Baeti Pass in Baekgok-myeon, Jincheon, Chungcheongbuk-do. Its terrain suited believers driven out of Naepo who crossed the mountains to live in hiding. In 1850 the first seminary of the Joseon vicariate, a minor seminary, was founded in this valley, so a hidden village was also a place that raised priests."

"Father Thomas Choe Yang-eop, Korea's second native priest, made this his pastoral base. Ordained in Shanghai in 1849, he spent twelve years walking to the villages of believers across the country until he died of exhaustion on the road in 1861 — which is why he is called a white martyr. The shrine holds a memorial displaying his life and letters, and is also known as the Father Choe Yang-eop Shrine."
