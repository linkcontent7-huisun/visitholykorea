"Mark Bak Seon-jin and Matthias Bak Tae-jin of Wonmeori were cousins. In 1868 the two were taken to Suwon, refused the demand to abandon their faith, and were martyred. Seo Deok-haeng of the same village recovered the bodies and buried them in their hometown, and the two martyrs' graves became a place remembered ever since by the believers of Wonmeori."

"Wonmeori in Hanjeong-ri, Sinpyeong-myeon, Dangjin, is a village of believers north of Sinpyeong Church. The village name is said to have changed from Eonduri, meaning the head of the seawall. Believers who gathered here to escape persecution made a living in this coastal village by making salt and firing earthenware."

"The Wonmeori graveyard holds the graves of Bak Seon-jin and Bak Tae-jin and a memorial honoring them. The remains, once moved to Sinpyeong Church, returned in 2009 to this original burial place, and the graveyard was renovated. A separate memorial remains at Sinpyeong Church, so the two places together convey the movement of the remains and their memory."
