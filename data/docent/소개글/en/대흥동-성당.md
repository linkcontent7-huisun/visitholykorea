"Daeheung-dong Church has its roots in Daejeon's community of believers, which grew from a small mission station. The Daejeon parish was founded in 1919 and moved to its present site in 1945, the year of liberation. Rebuilt after the church was lost in the Korean War, it stands as the central church of the Diocese of Daejeon, remembering both the wounds and the rebuilding of its community."

"The church sits in the old downtown of Daejeon's Jung-gu district. Its bell, ringing out among shops and lanes, once told residents the hours of the day. The nearby bakery Sungsimdang began by making steamed buns with two sacks of flour shared by the church. The story of church and bakery runs into the life of a city rising again after war."

"Completed in 1962, the church is known for a bell tower that recalls praying hands and a wide interior without pillars. Above the entrance are reliefs of the twelve apostles made jointly by Lee Nam-kyu and Choi Jong-tae. In the courtyard stands Lee Nam-kyu's statue of Mary, so the lines of a tall building and a sculpture with a human face can be seen together."
