"Baeron in Jecheon takes its name from a valley shaped like the bottom of a boat, and it was a village of believers formed during the persecutions. In 1801 Alexius Hwang Sa-yeong hid for eight months in an earthen cave used for firing pottery and wrote his letter on silk."

"St. Joseph's Seminary, founded at Baeron in 1855, was Korea's first modern seminary and began training clergy. Until it closed in the Byeongin Persecution of 1866, Korean seminarians studied Latin and theology there."

"Father Thomas Choe Yang-eop, Korea's second native priest, was buried at Baeron after his death in 1861. The shrine holds Hwang Sa-yeong's cave, the old seminary, and Father Choe's grave and memorial church, linking three histories of the early Church."
