"Hwang Gyeong-han was the son of Hwang Sa-yeong, executed in 1801 for the silk letter affair, and Mary Jeong Nan-ju, exiled to Jeju as a government slave. To spare at least her two-year-old son from slavery, Jeong Nan-ju left the child on the shore at Yecho-ri on Chuja Island."

"An old fisherman of the O family found the martyr's son on the shore at Sinyang-ri, Chuja Island, and took him in. Hwang Gyeong-han grew up on the island and left descendants, while his mother Jeong Nan-ju, in Daejeong on Jeju, never saw her son again."

"In 1999 the Diocese of Jeju designated as a shrine the grave of Hwang Gyeong-han on the hillside of Sinyang-ri and the area of the Cross of Tears at Yecho-ri. The grave overlooking the sea and the memorial cross on the shore link Jeong Nan-ju's sea route into exile with the parting of mother and son."
