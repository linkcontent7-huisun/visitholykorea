"Nambangje is where St. Joseph Cho Yun-ho was born in 1848, and the village of believers where his father, St. Peter Cho Hwa-seo, lived. Old Catholics from Domaji in Suwon, the family moved here after the grandfather, Andrew Cho, was martyred in the Gihae Persecution of 1839. From around 1850 Cho Hwa-seo traveled with Father Choe Yang-eop as his server, visiting hidden villages of believers across the country."

"The shrine lies in the fields of Sinchang-myeon, Asan, Chungcheongnam-do, not far from the Gokgyo stream. In the middle of the broad Naepo plain, this village suited believers fleeing persecution, who could farm and live unnoticed. The shrine still sits among rice paddies, so the village landscape the martyrs knew remains."

"In 1865 the two moved to Seongji-dong in Jeonju, and in December of the following year they were arrested. Because the law forbade executing father and son on the same day, Cho Hwa-seo died at Supjeongi on 13 December and Cho Yun-ho ten days later at Seocheongyo. Both were canonized on the same day in 1984, and in 2016 a monument marking a hundred and fifty years since their martyrdom was raised in this village."
